        Takeaway · Priority Checkpoint, a design recommendation

        Keep the person's priority at the final decision

        Keep the person's stated priorities available when making the final decision. If AI thinks another consideration should guide the choice, make that explicit so the person can evaluate it.

        Three checks for any AI workflow

        1. Check which option the AI actually chose, not just what its explanation mentions.
        2. Compare the final decision with and without explicitly repeating the user's priority.
        3. Reverse the priority and see whether the decision responds appropriately.

        What this does and doesn't show

        • One recorded run per case from a small development pilot: one model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) and seven constructed decisions. It shows what happened here, not how often it happens elsewhere.
        • Only handoff 1 explicitly received the original priority. Later handoffs could still discuss or paraphrase it.
        • The final decision received handoff 6, but not the original priority as a separate instruction. That doesn't mean everything about it was lost.
        • The reminder comparison uses the same handoff 6, plus one line; it doesn't repair the handoffs. Re-adding the priority at handoff 4 generated new handoffs 4–6, so that difference also includes ordinary generation variability.
        • A single changed choice is an observed difference, not proof of a reliable causal effect. We haven't identified why a choice changed, and we don't claim the model meant to override anyone.
        • Matching a stated priority is not the same as making the best decision. Following a reversed priority is an additional check, not proof of general alignment.
        • The Priority Checkpoint is a design recommendation drawn from these runs, not a validated universal rule.
        • Your predictions and your answer to the reflection question aren't measured, stored or used as research data.
