Anchor-Preservation Auditor · a research prototype supported by a Cosmos Institute grant

When AI agents make decisions on your behalf, does what matters to you survive the handoffs?

In our main study (Study 4), each decision was made twice, as a priority pair: once for a person who said A matters more than B, once for a person who said the reverse. A pair passes only if each person gets their own option. Told the priority directly, the final AI decision passed 24 of 24 pairs. After six AI handoffs: 6 of 48. With the priority restated at the final step: 47 of 48.

In short: after six AI handoffs, decisions became much less sensitive to the user's stated priority and resembled decisions made without one; restating it at the final step restored sensitivity. One model (Gemini 2.5 Pro), 12 constructed scenarios, 1,320 recorded calls: a controlled experiment, not a measure of how often this happens in your system.

Single decisions, and the no-priority comparison

Supporting evidence (exploratory)

  • Study 3C: deciding after each handoff of 28 saved chains, single decisions matched the priority after the first handoff (28 of 28) and much less often by handoffs 4–6 (7–9 of 28). Not a smooth decline; role and position are confounded.
  • Three other models on 14 published dilemmas (5 eligible for the priority-match measure): nearly all single decisions matched the priority when told directly (19–20 of 20) or restated (18–19 of 20), and after delegation the models chose much as they did without a priority. GPT-5.5 mostly declined, often writing "UNCLEAR." with a period, which the strict format counts as invalid; Claude Sonnet 5.5 picked the same option whichever priority was given.
  • A different workflow (exploratory): coordinator → two analysts → decider, on another model (Gemini 3.8 Flash), 7 constructed dilemmas. The coordinator usually passed the priority on, and decisions stayed sensitive to it in every dilemma (28 of 28 pairs; 6 decisions lost to a network outage). Model, number of handoffs and roles all differ from Study 4, so this shows that results can differ across workflows, not why. That is why the Auditor tests individual systems.
  • The earlier pilot (28 cases) showed the same pattern.

Quick Check · for your own AI chats

Got a recommendation from an AI? Ask it this before you decide.

Paste it into the same conversation where you told the AI what matters to you. No sign-up, no key, nothing sent from this page.

You can edit it first.

  • Untested. A suggested question, not a validated tool or detector. Our studies did not test this wording.
  • The reply isn't proof. What an AI says about its recommendation is not evidence of how it actually reached it.
  • New considerations can be good. The aim is to see them openly, not to reject them.
  • You decide. Judging the recommendation stays with you.
