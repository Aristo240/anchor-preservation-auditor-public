Map your workflow

Describe the agents in your workflow and what each one receives. The map shows where your priority is given directly, where it can only arrive inside another agent's output, and what is still unspecified.

What this does: applies fixed, visible rules to the structure you declare. What it does not do: run your agents, read model behaviour, or decide that a priority was lost because its words don't appear. A map is a description of your workflow's structure, not a behavioural evaluation of it: it shows where to test, not what a test would find. Nothing is uploaded or stored; it is gone on reload unless you export it.

    Trace inspection Declared versus observed

    The map above is what you declare. A trace recorded from one execution of your workflow shows what each agent's model request actually contained, and what framework state it could read. Put them side by side to find routes you didn't intend.

    What this can show: where the priority's exact words were present, and where a recorded request contains another agent's output. What it can't show: that the priority's meaning was absent. It may be paraphrased, or your instrumentation may not capture everything. Anything not captured is shown as unknown. One trace is one execution: inspection, not a behavioural test. The trace is read in this tab only.

    Traces come from priority_check workflow runs (save one execution's trace on Inspect and compare) or from your own instrumentation in the format apa-trace/1. The example is a four-agent LangGraph system run with an offline fake model.

    Discrepancies and gaps

      Behavioural evaluation Next: test whether the final decision changes with the priority

      A map can't show whether the decision uses the priority. A test can: ask the same decision with the priority and with the opposite priority, with and without it repeated at the deciding step.

      • Your whole workflow, as it runs: connect it with a small adapter and run priority_check workflow. It runs YOUR system with the priority, with the opposite priority and without it, in both option orders, with fresh state each time, and scores the final decision. Conditions your system can't support are reported as not supported. See the kit's docs/WORKFLOW_ADAPTER.md and the LangGraph example.
      • Your own deciding step: For developers gives you the two prompts to run by hand (with and without one line repeating the priority). The priority_check run command automates the same comparison with your own adapter.
      • Our reference workflow on your decision: fill in the two options below and save a scenario file for priority_check chain. It runs our fixed six-handoff reference workflow (advisor → critic → summarizer, twice), not your own agents, even if your map above has a different structure. Its result tells you how that reference workflow behaves with a model on your decision, not how your system behaves.
      • A recorded run of your own workflow: paste its handoffs into Review a trace. That supports inspection; on its own it is not a controlled experiment.
