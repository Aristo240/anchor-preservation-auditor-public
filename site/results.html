Results · Study 4 first

What the studies found

After six AI handoffs, decisions became much less sensitive to the user's stated priority and resembled decisions made without an explicit priority. Restating the priority at the final step restored sensitivity in this controlled experiment.

Study 4 (primary): Gemini 2.5 Pro, 12 constructed scenarios

Each scenario under 2 opposite priorities, in 2 option orders, with 2 independent handoff chains. A unit is paired-sensitive only if both opposite priorities got their own option.

With no priority at all, the model declined in 33% of direct decisions and 38% of decisions after a six-handoff chain; after six handoffs with a priority given at the start, it declined in 35%. With the priority stated, it never declined. This is a descriptive comparison, not a causal one; it concerns one model and one family of constructed scenarios, and matching a restated priority shows instruction-following, not better decisions.

  • Coordinator workflow (exploratory): coordinator → two analysts → decider, Gemini 3.8 Flash, 7 constructed dilemmas. Workflow decisions stayed sensitive to the priority: 56 of 56 single decisions matched, 28 of 28 pairs. Direct and restated: 54 of 56 matched, with 2 missing each after a network outage. The coordinator usually relayed the priority's exact wording, which the analysts then received. Two dilemmas were completed in a documented technical-recovery run approved after the first five dilemmas' results were known, so it is not an uninterrupted preregistered run. Model, handoff count and roles differ from Study 4: results can differ across workflows.

Earlier development pilot (Study 3B, 28 cases)

An earlier, smaller pilot that showed the same pattern. 7 constructed decisions, each under 2 opposing priorities and in 2 option orders, each decided four ways: told directly, and three ways after six handoffs. Everything below this point is the pilot.

A small development pilot: one model, one run per case, described rather than tested statistically. It shows what happened in these cases, not how often it happens elsewhere.

Pilot, the stricter test: did the choice follow the priority both ways?

One choice can match a priority by coincidence. For each decision and option order, a condition counts only if both opposing priorities produced their own matching option.
